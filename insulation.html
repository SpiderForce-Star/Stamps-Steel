Why Insulate a PEB

Comfort, condensation, and code

Steel buildings sweat and they dump heat. Insulation is how you keep the shell quiet, dry, and usable year-round in Middle Tennessee.

Two systems cover almost every shop, barn, and commercial PEB we quote. The first is the one most buildings actually get. The second is how you seriously raise R-value.

  • · Cuts heat gain and heat loss through the shell
  • · Helps control condensation on metal panels
  • · Gives the interior a finished look
  • · Supports energy-code and conditioned-space goals

Most Popular System

Blanket Insulation Over the Purlin

White-faced / vinyl-reinforced facing. Stock thicknesses: 3″, 4″, and 6″.

What most buildings get

How it goes together: faced blanket over the Z, sandwiched under the panel at the flange. White facing drapes in the bay. The bay is not filled.

This is the insulation package on most pre-engineered metal buildings. A fiberglass blanket with a white, vinyl-reinforced facing is laid over the purlins (roof) and girts (walls) before the panels go on. The panels sandwich the blanket at the flange. The white facing looks into the building — it is the finished ceiling and wall look, and the vapor retarder.

White vinyl-reinforced facing

The facing is a white, vinyl-reinforced vapor retarder. It is the interior finish and the moisture control layer. Reinforcement helps it survive handling and erection.

  • · Bright white interior
  • · Reinforced against tear-outs
  • · Vapor retarder for condensation control
  • · Standard on most PEB packages

Spec note: this facing is a reinforced vinyl / PSK-style vapor retarder. Heavier puncture-resistant facings are available for high-abuse interiors (gyms, livestock, busy industrial).

Higher R-Value Option

Between the Purlin — Unfaced, Into the Cavity

Still goes over the purlin. Unfaced. Then the purlin / girt cavity is filled so R-value jumps. Same method on the walls.

When you need more R

Unfaced over the Z and packed into the bay. White facing / liner is the underside.

Install view: unfaced fill packed in the purlin cavity. A liner / banding can finish the underside.

The second system also goes over the purlins and girts — but the blanket is unfaced. Instead of one faced layer compressed under the panel, unfaced insulation is placed over the members and into the purlin / girt cavity so that space is filled.

Filling the cavity is how you seriously increase R-value beyond a 3″ / 4″ / 6″ faced blanket. Use it when the building is conditioned, code-driven, or you want the shell to work harder year-round.

Over the member

Unfaced across purlins and girts

Unfaced fiberglass still runs over the structural members so steel flanges are covered — not left as a thermal short.

Into the cavity

Fill between purlins and girts

The same unfaced product drops into the bay between members. That filled depth is the R-value jump.

A separate interior liner or banding can finish the underside when the job needs a clean look. That is a finish option — not the name of the system.
