MBMA-informed practices

What that means on your job

The MBMA Common Industry Practices (Metal Building Systems Manual, Chapter IV) describe how metal building systems are typically designed, furnished, and erected. Key points we plan around:

  • ·Erection drawings from the manufacturer suggest sequence and connections; the erector selects safe methods and complies with OSHA and local rules.
  • ·True, level, plumb frames with temporary and permanent bracing for stability during and after erection.
  • ·Anchorages checked before setting columns; bearing plates leveled with shims/grout when required.
  • ·Insulation & cladding installed per system details — vapor control, base conditions, and roof-to-wall interfaces matter for performance and air leakage.

Reference: MBMA Common Industry Practices / Metal Building Systems Manual — educational summary only; not a substitute for project engineering or the official manual.

From truck to turn-key shell

Erection process

Typical sequence for a pre-engineered metal building package. Exact order follows your manufacturer drawings and site conditions.

1

Site readiness & layout

Confirm slab or piers are complete, cured, and accessible for crane/forklift. Verify anchor bolt pattern, projection, and template alignment against erection drawings. Establish laydown areas, power, and safety zones.

2

Unload from flatbed trucks

Receive primary frames, secondary (purlins/girts), bracing, panels, trim, insulation, and hardware. Stage materials in erection sequence — protect panels and insulation from damage and weather. Inventory against shipping lists.

3

Heavy primary frame erection

Set columns and rigid-frame rafters (crane or telehandler as required). Plumb and level frames; install temporary bracing for stability. Complete bolted connections per drawings. Erect endwall frames and intermediate bays in a controlled sequence so the structure stays stable at every stage.

4

Permanent bracing & secondary framing

Install rod/cable or angle bracing (flange braces, diagonal bracing) as designed. Set eave struts, purlins (roof secondary), and girts (wall secondary). Check alignment and bay spacing before sheeting — secondary framing carries cladding loads and keeps the building square.

5

Insulation installation

Install the insulation system specified for your package — for example single-layer faced blanket draped over purlins/girts, double-layer systems, filled cavity, or liner systems used with standing-seam roofs (common approaches described in MBMA energy guidance). Maintain vapor retarder orientation, overlaps, and base seals. Coordinate insulation with panel installation so blankets are not damaged or left exposed.

6

Sheeting — roof & walls

Install roof panels first in many sequences (protect structure from weather), then wall panels — or as directed by drawings. Through-fastened or standing-seam systems are set with proper lap, fasteners, and sealants. Maintain square lines at corners, eaves, and rakes for clean trim fit.

7

Trim, flashings & closures

Base trim, corner trim, eave trim, rake/gable trim, ridge, gutters/downspouts (if in package), and foam or metal closures. Critical for weather-tightness and finished appearance — roof-to-wall interfaces get special attention for air and water control.

8

Accessories & openings

Walk doors, overhead/roll-up doors, windows, louvers, vents, cupolas, canopies, and other accessories included with your building. Frame openings, flash properly, and complete hardware so the shell is ready for your interior fit-out.

What’s typically included

Scope is confirmed on every proposal. Common erection package elements:

Unload & stage

Primary steel

Columns, rafters, endwalls — plumb, level, bolted, and temporarily braced.

Secondary steel

Insulation

Sheeting & trim
