Aircraft Hangars

Personal hangars & multi-plane T-hangars

Clear-span bays and door openings for one aircraft or a T-hangar row — sized for your airport or private strip.

Personal / single hangars

One clear-span bay sized to your aircraft’s wingspan, height, and taxi path. Common options include hydraulic or lift-strap bifold doors that open upward into a canopy, tall sectional overhead doors, or sliding hangar doors. High eaves clear tails and propellers; insulation and lined interiors are available for climate control and workshop use.

  • · Door clear opening matched to wingspan + margin
  • · Bifold, hydraulic, sectional, or sliding door packages
  • · Concrete apron / taxilane interface design support
  • · Private strip or airport-compliant PEB packages

T-hangars & multi-plane rows

T-hangars use nested T-shaped floor plans so more aircraft fit per acre than freestanding boxes. Each pilot gets a private stall and door onto a shared taxilane. Continuous multi-unit PEB rows (nested T or nested box) are ideal for FBOs, airport authorities, and developers building leaseable hangar inventory.

  • · Nested T layouts for maximum aircraft density
  • · Individual stall doors · shared taxilanes
  • · Scalable multi-building campuses
  • · Coordinated panels, colors, and door systems

Hangar door note

Bifold and hydraulic hangar doors (lift-strap bifold, one-piece hydraulic, and similar systems) are specialty door products. Stamps Steel furnishes the engineered PEB shell with framed openings sized for your chosen door manufacturer — we coordinate clear height, wind loads, and header design so the door package and building work as one system. Tell us aircraft type, door preference, and site codes when you request a quote.

Every project starts with the right frame. Clear-span or modular, single slope or lean-to — primary steel, purlins, girts, and framed openings define how your building works.

Frame types

Straight & tapered clear span, 1–3 row modular, single slope, and lean-tos — matched to width, clear height, and use.

Purlins & wall girts

Secondary members that carry roof and wall panels into the primary frames — Z-purlins, girts, eave struts.

Framed openings

Overhead doors, walk doors, windows, louvers, and roof vents — headers and jambs integrated with the wall system.
